Compress the cache, don't share it

Grouped-query attention shrinks the KV cache by sharing key/value heads — which throws away head diversity and costs a little quality. DeepSeek's Multi-head Latent Attention shrinks it by compression: cache one small low-rank latent per token and reconstruct full per-head keys and values on the fly. The cache gets smaller than even GQA, and the quality matches full attention.

read  ~16 min math  low-rank KV interactive  cache · pipeline series  Attention · 16

Grouped-query attention shrank the KV cache by giving many query heads a shared set of key/value heads. It works, but sharing is lossy: fewer distinct K/V heads means less expressive attention, so GQA trades a little quality for its cache savings. DeepSeek-V2 (2024) asked whether you can shrink the cache without giving up per-head diversity — and answered with Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA).

The move is to stop caching keys and values at all. Instead, project each token's hidden state down into a small latent vector, cache that, and reconstruct the full per-head keys and values from it whenever attention runs. You cache a compressed seed; each head regenerates the detail it needs.

The one idea

MLA replaces the KV cache with a low-rank latent. Down-project the token to a small vector ccc of dimension dc≪dmodeld_c \ll d_{\text{model}}dc​≪dmodel​, cache only ccc, and up-project it back to full keys and values per head at attention time. Because the full head-specific K/V are reconstructed (not shared as in GQA), head diversity is preserved — so the cache shrinks past GQA and quality stays at MHA level. Compression beats sharing.

The RoPE snag, and the decoupling fix

There is a subtle obstacle. The absorption trick — folding the up-projection into the query so you attend in latent space — needs the key to be a plain linear function of the cached latent. But rotary position embeddings (RoPE) apply a position-dependent rotation to the key, and that rotation cannot be absorbed into a query projection because it depends on the relative position of query and key. Low-rank KV compression and RoPE are, out of the box, incompatible.

DeepSeek's fix is decoupled RoPE: split the key into two parts — a compressed part from the latent (no RoPE) and a small, separate part that carries the RoPE rotation, shared across heads. The query gets a matching decoupled RoPE component. The cache then holds the latent ctc_tct​ plus that small RoPE key ktRk^R_tktR​ (of dimension dRd_RdR​) — still tiny. Why it wins on both axes

The reported numbers are striking because they beat GQA on cache while matching MHA on quality — the two usually trade off. In DeepSeek-V2, MLA's cache is roughly 4–14% of MHA's (smaller for larger models), and DeepSeek-V3 reports on the order of 70 KB/token versus ~192–328 KB/token for GQA-based models — a 2.7–4.7× reduction over GQA — while maintaining performance comparable to (and in their ablations, better than) standard multi-head attention. That combination is what makes MLA the KV-cache method of choice for the largest efficient models: it powers DeepSeek-V2 and the 671B DeepSeek-V3.

Sharing vs compression, precisely GQA reduces the number of K/V heads — a coarse, lossy quantization of head diversity. MLA reduces the dimension via low-rank projection and then reconstructs full per-head K/V — it keeps the diversity and only compresses the shared information content. Same goal (smaller cache), fundamentally different lever, and compression turns out to dominate.

What it costs

  • Extra compute to reconstruct. Up-projecting the latent back to K/V (or absorbing it into the query) is more arithmetic than GQA's simple sharing — usually a good trade, since decode is memory-bound, but not free.
  • The decoupled-RoPE machinery is intricate. Splitting keys and queries into compressed and RoPE-carrying parts is more complex to implement and reason about than dropping heads.
  • It's an architecture, not a retrofit. You cannot cheaply uptrain an existing MHA checkpoint into MLA the way you can into GQA; MLA has to be designed in (though conversion methods are being explored).

Where this sits in the lineage

MLA is the KV-cache-reduction branch evolved one step: from sharing heads (MQA/GQA) to compressing them (low-rank latent). It rhymes with Linformer — both are low-rank compression — but along a different axis: Linformer compresses the sequence dimension, MLA compresses the head/feature dimension of the cache. Combined with mixture-of-experts, MLA is a big part of why frontier models like DeepSeek-V3 and Kimi-scale systems can serve enormous parameter counts at long context without the KV cache eating the GPU. The frontier's other move — replacing the growing cache with a fixed-size state — is the linear-attention line that leads to Kimi K3.
